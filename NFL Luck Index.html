Winning vs. Luck, 2022–2026

Each team's win % since 2022 against its total luck wins

This graph shows all NFL teams ranked by win percentage since 2022 on the x-axis, compared to the total added luck wins on the y-axis. “Added Luck Wins” is my new metric that essentially attempts to calculate how many wins or losses per team have been made up by luck, through the lens of a few different ways to quantify luck within the NFL.

I will get more into the specifics of how each of these different methods works, but the main categories I identified were missed kicks against a team (excluding blocks), subjective penalties for or against a team (excluding procedural or objective penalties), interception-worthy throws by a team that did not result in interceptions, and strength-of-schedule luck, which essentially compared how hard a team’s schedule would be expected to be at the beginning of the year versus how hard it ended up being. “Added Luck Wins” comes from the sum of estimated wins gained from each of these categories per team.

The four sources of luck

Missed Kick Luck

Opponents' missed kicks, 2022–2026

Expected points added (EPA) is the change in the expected outcome of the drive as a result of any given play, measured in how many points a drive is expected to produce. Win percentage added (WPA) is the change in win percentage for the team with the ball as a result of any given play. For this graph, I multiplied both by -1 to find the epa and wpa gained by the defense, as they are the ones benefitting from the missed kicks. This graph measures the total sum of epa and wpa, respectively, on all plays where a team had a kick missed against them (excluding blocks). Every 100% of win percentage added was classified as 1 win added. Because every team benefits from some missed kicks in a normal season, the league average (about 1.77 wins) was subtracted from each team's total before it was added into the Added Luck Wins metric, so this category measures how much more or less often kicks were missed against a team compared to an average team. This chart reflects the values before that subtraction, but that is what was used for the overall metric.

Penalty Luck

Judgment-call flags, 2022–2026

The x and y axes work the same way as the missed kick luck axes, using expected points added and win percentage added, and each 100% of win percentage added or subtracted in total equating to 1 win being added or subtracted. In the selection of which penalties involved some form of luck, I ignored any that were procedural or objective, something like a false start. I ended up including 12 penalties within the scope, with the majority of penalties in the dataset being holding or pass interferences, whether on the offense or defense.

Dropped Interception Luck

Interception-worthy throws not intercepted, 2022–2026

“Dropped” interception luck may be a bit of a misleading title, but this graph essentially encapsulates the results of FTN's "interception-worthy" throws per team (from FTN's play-by-play charting data, available through nflverse) that were not actually intercepted. The y-axis is the percentage of those interception-worthy throws that, in fact, were not intercepted, while the x-axis is the EPA saved by it not being an interception versus if it had been. The “wins added” coming from this stat were acquired by finding the average win percentage lost on all interceptions since 2022 within the dataset, about 10%, and then the average win percentage lost on all non-intercepted interception-worthy throws, about 2%. This means there is about 8%, or 0.08, saved from every one of these throws not being intercepted. To find how lucky each team was, I compared its rate of non-intercepted interception-worthy throws to the league-average rate of about 50%. The difference, multiplied by that team's number of interception-worthy throws, gives the number of interceptions it avoided above or below what an average team would have. That number was then multiplied by 0.08 to find the total win percentage saved, or total wins added per team.

Schedule Luck

Opponent strength vs. preseason expectations, 2022–2026

This metric attempts to measure how difficult a team’s schedule was supposed to be going into the season versus how hard it actually was. This could be found in 2 ways. The x-axis, “Schedule luck gained from opponents' actual records," measures the sum of a team’s opponents’ projected win totals for a given year minus the sum of those opponents’ actual wins for the year. Essentially, how many total wins a team was expected to face versus how many they actually faced. The y-axis is a little more complicated. For each opponent a team faces, the pregame market win percentage for the other 16 games that opponent played is averaged, giving you a dynamic expected winning percentage for that opponent. Then, that expected winning percentage is subtracted from that opponent’s preseason expected winning percentage, using the same projected win totals as the x-axis, to find the difference in how good this opponent was expected to be versus how good they actually were. For each team, this process is repeated for every opponent they face, and then those values are summed to find the total “wins added” by strength of schedule luck.

Takeaways and Next Steps

  • This metric is not perfect, nor even close. There are many variables outside the scope of this research, such as injuries, as well as confounding variables within it that contribute to how lucky any NFL team is. I just wanted to give my best effort in finding a way to quantify that aspect of a sport I love.
  • There is an interesting trend in the overall luck wins added metric, in that the 5 teams with the best winning percentage since 2022, as well as the 5 teams with the worst winning percentage in that time, all fall within the bottom 15 in the “added luck wins” metric. This goes against the theory that I had going into the project, that teams with higher winning percentages would naturally have more added luck wins due to having more wins in general. This also doesn’t fully align with the two variables being completely disconnected, as you would therefore expect more variability out of the two extremes, rather than a surge in added luck wins in the middle of the winning percentage rankings. Despite lots of brainstorming, I couldn’t find a concrete reason for why this would be. If you are seeing this project and have an inclination to why this trend might be visible in the data, please reach out to me, as I would love to discuss this further.
  • The results of which teams are considered lucky versus unlucky have been very interesting, and surprising in parts, for me to look at. As someone who watches lots of NFL, going into this project, I was expecting the Vikings and Steelers to chart high on this metric. The 2022 Vikings are the team I would predict to have the most wins added off of pure luck, and it’s felt like they have had multiple years of padded win totals. The Steelers, similarly, always seem to find a way to just barely get over .500, no matter the skill level of the team. On the flip side, it is very surprising to see the Browns and Giants high up on this list, as both franchises have seemed to be plagued with bad luck. Similarly, the Chiefs, a team commonly viewed as lucky or favored by the refs, finish 29th in the added luck wins metric, along with being below average in win percentage added from subjective penalties. This goes against the common stigma you hear against the team, an interesting finding from the project.
  • Next Steps: I want to take this metric and become increasingly holistic with it. I would like to account for more of the variables that go into determining how lucky a team is. Looking at how injuries affect teams based on that player’s production would be an interesting next step to take. Additionally, I would like to break down the existing data further. I am working on visualizations that show year-by-year results per team, allowing me to find the luckiest seasons in the NFL within the past 5 years.
